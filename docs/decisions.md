# Decisions

## Game server (2026-10-20, crates reworked 2026-10-21)
- Crates are shared world crates, not per-player spawns. A seeder worker (`seedWorldCrates`, every 5 min) keeps `LOTUS_CRATES_PER_CITY` (60) active crates alive in each launch city; a crate belongs to nobody, the first player to reach one opens it, and that takes it off the map for everyone. 12 h TTL, replaced by the seeder.
- Nothing is ever placed because a particular player turned up. Crates exist only inside the launch cities in `cities.ts`, and a player outside them has no crates — the map says so rather than quietly spawning one.
- Why: the old design spawned up to 40 crates per player per day, so the prize cost grew with every player added. With a fixed set of crates the day's payout is bounded by how many crates exist, not by how many people play. Dials: `LOTUS_CRATES_PER_CITY`, `LOTUS_MAX_CRATES_PER_DAY` (hard global ceiling on crates created per UTC day, default 1500), `LOTUS_CRATE_TTL_MIN`.
- Ten free opens per player per UTC day (`LOTUS_FREE_OPENS_PER_DAY`), each at a 1% card rate (0008_ten_opens_one_percent.sql). Every further open that day spends a ticket and rolls on the better `ticket` odds (5%). Enforced by a conditional write on `player_open_days` in the open transaction, so two simultaneous opens can't both take the last free one or the last ticket.
- Why ten at 1% rather than one at 10%: the same cost per player per day, but a reason to keep walking instead of a game you touch once. The daily prize ceiling, not the open count, is what bounds the spend.
- Tickets are spent by opening, not by a button, so a ticket can't be wasted on a crate the player never reaches. 25 shards convert to a ticket while tickets < 5, and spending one frees room for banked shards to convert. 25 (not 10) because ten opens a day already bank about 20 shards: at 10 a ticket a day would be automatic and tickets would set the card rate instead of the street crates.
- Daily prize budget as a circuit breaker: `LOTUS_MAX_CARD_VALUE_PER_DAY` (400 USD, aryan's number on 2026-10-21) and `LOTUS_MAX_CARDS_PER_DAY` (25). The dollar ceiling is meant to be the one that binds; the card count is a sanity stop for a vault full of cheap cards, so it sits above what $400 of average cards comes to. Each card is booked against `prize_budget_days` inside the open transaction, so the ceiling is exact even when wins land at the same instant; a card that would go over is put back under a SAVEPOINT. Past either ceiling the card tiers roll 0 and opens pay shards, and `getPublicOdds` reports that, so the published odds never promise what can't be paid.
- Coin holder gate (2026-10-21, aryan: "ik now the user has to hold some of the coin right"): a wallet must hold `LOTUS_HOLD_REQUIRED_USD` (25) of our coin to open a crate. Checked before the open transaction, since it needs the chain and a price feed.
- The gate is dormant until a coin address is set, and `coinHoldConfig()` returning null is the only switch. `COIN_MINT` or the site's `NEXT_PUBLIC_COIN_ADDRESS` turns it on, so launch day is one setting; the site's placeholder text isn't base58 and reads as no coin. Before the coin exists there is nothing to hold, so a missing or malformed address means no requirement rather than an error. With an address but no `SOLANA_RPC_URL` it falls back to the public RPC rather than silently staying off: a gate that quietly lets everyone in is the worse failure.
- A qualifying check opens a 48 h window (`holder_windows`, one row per player) and snapshots the required token amount, so a price move during the window never changes what somebody has to hold. Inside the window the balance is re-read at most every `LOTUS_HOLD_RECHECK_MIN` (10 min); finding it under the snapshot sets `locked_out_at` and pauses opens until the window ends, and topping back up does not clear it. This is what keeps the cost at about two HTTP calls per player per day instead of two per open.
- The window is keyed on the player, not the wallet, so moving the coin to a second wallet cannot clear a lockout.
- Fails closed: if the RPC or the price feed can't be read the open is refused (`coin_unavailable`) and nothing is spent — no daily open, no ticket, and the crate stays on the map. An outage does not set a lockout either, since it is not the player's fault.
- Being under the requirement opens no window, so buying the coin and trying again works immediately.
- Losing a race is not a punishment: the loser keeps their daily open and anti-cheat hands back the cooldown and daily quota (`ValidateResult.release`).
- Walkable placement (`server/src/walkable.ts`): crates land on OpenStreetMap footways, pedestrian streets, paths, parks, gardens, playgrounds and plazas, never in lakes/rivers/coast (12 m buffer), and no two active crates within 120 m (`LOTUS_CRATE_SPACING_M`) so a cluster can't be cleared from one spot. Motorways, roads, crossings, bridges, tunnels and private-access ways are never queried.
- Source is the public Overpass API (no account or key), two mirrors, queried per ~400 m grid cell, cached 6 h in memory (600 cells), requests spaced 600 ms apart. The seeder makes at most `CRATE_SEED_MAX_ANCHORS` (8) lookups per tick and converges over several ticks. Set `OVERPASS_URLS` to point at a self-hosted or paid instance; `WALKABLE_SPAWNS=off` disables it. Tests skip it unless `WALKABLE_SPAWNS=on`.
- If Overpass is slow, down or rate limiting the seeder places nothing and logs it: an empty patch of map beats a crate in a lake or on a motorway. Crates already out live 12 h, which gives the next ticks time. After all mirrors fail Overpass is skipped for 60 s. Known gaps: multipolygon holes and unclosed relation rings are ignored, buildings are not excluded inside parks.
- Reading the map creates nothing, so a location ping is one indexed query with no Overpass call in the request path.
- Open radius 40 m, enforced by core in SQL (`ST_DWithin`) inside the open transaction. Movement/cooldowns/caps belong to anti-cheat.
- Empty crate pays 1–3 shards. Empty tier at roll time downgrades to shards, never a lower card tier.
- Prize sends use a transactional outbox (`prize_sends`), written in the same transaction as the win.
- Known trade: a player with no crate within walking distance has nothing to do that day, and in a busy city the ten opens may not all be reachable. `LOTUS_CRATES_PER_CITY` is the dial; raising it costs nothing in prizes, because the daily prize ceiling is unchanged by it.

## Treasury (2026-10-20)
- Design: Squads safe holds funds and most cards; the hot wallet holds about a day of prizes. Bots may only propose Squads transactions or call permissionless pump.fun instructions; signers approve every move out of the safe. Full write-up in the "CardQuest treasury and money flow" doc.
- `inventory_items.location` (`hot`/`vault`, migration 0003). Vault cards are tracked but never awardable; inventory sync moves them to `hot`/`available` once they land in the hot wallet (confirmed on chain, not just DAS).
- `SQUADS_VAULT_ADDRESS` (optional, read-only) lets inventory sync report vault cards that neither wallet holds.
- Card buying stays manual at launch. The admin form records cost and purchase tx, which writes a `purchase` treasury event.
- Plan prize math at pump.fun's documented creator fee: 0.30% before graduation, and 0.95% once the coin is on PumpSwap, stepping down as market cap grows. Re-check the live pump.fun fee page before launch, since pump.fun sets these.
- Do not enable "Holder Rewards" at launch. Fee sharing is set to 100% to the Squads vault, and that setting is one-time and permanent.
- Top-ups are one Squads batch proposal per top-up (one approval moves many cards). Drafted by an Initiate-only key; the worker refuses a key that can vote or execute.
- Fee claiming uses pump.fun's official SDK and only the permissionless distribution path, so it needs a fee-sharing config with the vault as a shareholder.
- Treasury events are written by the code that causes them (purchase form, top-up execution, fee claim) rather than by a chain watcher.
